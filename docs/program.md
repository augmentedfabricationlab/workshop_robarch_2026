Three days, structured as one loop repeated at increasing resolution: **read the damage → propose a repair → execute it → measure what actually happened**.

## Day 1 — Reading the structure

| Time | Phase |
|------|-------|
| 09:00–10:00 | Introduction: damage as prompt, the Reasoning-to-Action workflow |
| 10:00–11:00 | Tour of the Fachwerk wall and the damaged beams |
| 11:00–13:00 | Capture: photographs, notes, measurements, scans |
| 14:00–17:00 | Build the Assembly Model — components, connections, damage states |

**Deliverable.** A verified Assembly Model of the wall segment, with at least one damage state described well enough to act on.

## Day 2 — From strategy to toolpath

| Time | Phase |
|------|-------|
| 09:00–11:00 | Structured prompting: roles, repair goals, tool and material constraints |
| 11:00–13:00 | Generate and compare at least two alternative repair strategies |
| 14:00–16:00 | Joint selection and parametrisation; derive the cut geometry |
| 16:00–17:00 | Simulation and validation of candidate toolpaths |

**Deliverable.** An Action Model per team: an explicit, ordered repair sequence with each step allocated to human or robot.

## Day 3 — Execution and evaluation

| Time | Phase |
|------|-------|
| 09:00–12:00 | Robotic execution: scanning, marking, milling |
| 12:00–14:00 | Manual execution: material removal, surface treatment, fitting |
| 14:00–15:30 | Scan-after-cut: measure the result against the model |
| 15:30–17:00 | Comparative presentation and discussion |

**Deliverable.** A repaired element, a scan of what was actually produced, and the digital record of how the decision was reached.

## The loop

Each cycle passes through four points where judgment sits with the participant, not the model:

1. **Framing.** What gets photographed, measured, and named as damage is already an interpretation.
2. **Verification.** The Assembly Model is a hypothesis. It gets checked against the beam in front of you.
3. **Comparison.** The workflow proposes alternatives; weighing them against feasibility, care for the existing material, and future use is a human call.
4. **Evaluation.** The scan-after-cut loop closes the gap between the idealised model and real, scanned timber.
