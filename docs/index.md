# Actionable Repair

**Detangling Dependencies · ROB|ARCH 2026 · Workshop 04**

*What if damage were a prompt: what repair strategies might it generate, and which of them can a robot actually carry out?*

This workshop puts multimodal AI and human–robot cooperation to work on a concrete repair task. It connects high-level repair design to situated robotic perception, planning, and action through a **Multimodal Reasoning-to-Action workflow**: textual observations, photographic documentation, and geometry of an existing structure are translated into a structured **Assembly Model** describing components, connections, and damage states.

That model is the substrate for exploring repair designs and deriving an **Action Model**, a structured repair sequence in which human- and robot-executable steps are made explicit. Designs are produced through structured prompting with defined roles, repair goals, and constraints: available tools, materials, and robotic capabilities.

Robotic routines handle the operations that benefit from precision, scanning, marking, and milling. Complementary steps, material removal, surface treatment, and assembly, stay in the hands of the participants.

## The case study

Participants work on a curated set of damaged beams in a **half-timbered (Fachwerk) wall**. Repair interventions are prototyped through iterative cycles of planning, manual and robotic execution, and evaluation. The workshop ends with a repaired structure and a documented digital record of the workflows and outcomes.

## Expected outcomes

- **Executable strategies** — distinct, repeatable repair instructions for human and robot execution
- **Robot toolpaths** — candidate paths generated with LLM support, validated in simulation
- **Physical prototypes** — repaired artefacts and one structural element scenario
- **Digital records** — saved Assembly and Action Models, plus documentation
