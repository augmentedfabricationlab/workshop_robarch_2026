# PJ4 – Cheek Patch

DE: Wangenflicken · JP: katagi-ire

Half the section removed over a bounded length and replaced. The deepest
patch in the set, and the boundary case: any deeper and the member is severed
and a splice is needed instead.

`kind: "patch"` · restores **section** · does not carry along the member

## Geometry

Three half-space cuts, one intersect group: floor on the centre plane, bounded
in y, full width, open at the +z face.

| | canonical | of section |
|---|---|---|
| depth | 0.50 | 50% — half the section |
| length | 1.60 | 1.6 × |
| width | full | 100% |

Kept fraction **0.730**.

## When to reach for it

Decay that reaches the core but not through it, over a length. It restores
section — the repaired member has its full area again — but **not
continuity**: the grain is cut at both shoulders, so it transfers no tension
along the beam and only what the remaining half-depth can carry in bending.

That distinction decides between PJ4 and a splice. If the sound half is
sufficient for the load, PJ4 keeps 73% of the timber. If it is not, no patch
will do and SJ1–SJ8 are the answer however wasteful they look.

## Fabrication

One long rip along the centre plane and two cross-grain shoulders — the same
cuts as a half lap, but stopped at both ends, which makes the rip a stopped
cut and much harder than SJ1's. Bore the corners, saw in from both shoulders,
waste out between.

The stopped rip is the reason to think twice: it cannot be run with a hand
saw from either end, and in situ it wants a router or a chain mortiser.
