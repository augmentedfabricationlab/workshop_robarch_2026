# SJ1 – Half Lap Joint

DE: Gerades Blatt · JP: koshi-kake-tsugi (腰掛継ぎ)

The plainest splice in the chapter: the section is divided at mid-height and
each half carries a square-shouldered tongue one thickness long. No bevels,
no chevron, no taper — every cut is square to a face. Both parts are
identical (the book marks it "2×"), so one setup produces both sides.

The two elevations are mirror images about mid-height, which is what the
"2×" mark asserts, and the measurement confirms it to within a pixel.

Canonical placement: `aspect` 3.0 with the shoulder at y = 1.0 and the
tongue end at y = 2.0, giving the measured lap of one thickness with a
thickness of solid beam either side. This matches SJ3's convention, whose
chevron runs between the same stations.

## Purpose

The default repair splice where a beam is sound on both sides of a local
defect and there is room to work along the length. It is the joint to reach
for first: fastest to cut, easiest to mark, and the one whose failure mode
is best understood. Everything else in the chapter is this joint with an
angle added somewhere.

## Structural behaviour

Shear passes across the lap plane, and in bending the joint carries only
what the two half-sections can carry — roughly a quarter of the intact beam
in the plane of the lap, since each half has half the depth. It has
essentially no tension capacity on its own: nothing but friction resists the
halves sliding apart, so it must be pegged, bolted or strapped. The square
shoulder concentrates stress at the internal corner and leaves short grain
at the tongue root, which is precisely the weakness the mitered (SJ6) and
spear point (SJ3) variants exist to soften.

Being point-symmetric it has no handedness, and the insertion cone is wide:
the halves separate by lifting apart across the lap plane as readily as by
drawing along it. That is convenient to assemble and the reason it needs
mechanical fastening.

## Fabrication – sequence and tools

Tools: try square, marking gauge, marking knife; ryoba or backsaw; wide
paring chisel; mallet.

1. **Lay out** (A1) – gauge the lap plane at mid-height right around the
   piece, and square the shoulder line across all four faces.
2. **Shoulder cut** (A2) – crosscut down at the shoulder line, stopping
   exactly on the gauge line. Cut on the waste side.
3. **Cheek cut** (A3) – rip from the end face along the lap plane until the
   waste block frees itself at the shoulder kerf.
4. **Pare** – flatten the cheek and square the shoulder to the lines. A
   cheek that is hollow is fine; one that is proud will hold the joint open.
5. **Assemble** (A+A → AA) – the second part is identical, rotated end for
   end. Close it, then peg or bolt through the lap.

## Effort

Two saw cuts and a little paring per part, both from the same layout. The
lowest-effort joint in the catalogue and the only one that can reasonably be
cut in situ with hand tools alone.
