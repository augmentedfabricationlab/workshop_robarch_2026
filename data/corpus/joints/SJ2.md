# SW2 – Stehendes gerades Blatt mit beidseitig schrägem Stoß

EN: Half lap joint with mitered ends · JP: ai-sogi-tsugi (相殺継ぎ)

## Purpose
General splice for moderate damage. Both halves are IDENTICAL (the joint is
point-symmetric, "2x" in the catalogue) – a single part profile for both
sides simplifies fabrication and replacement. The lap stands vertical
(dividing the width); set it lying via `rotation` = 90°.

## Structural behaviour
The half lap transfers shear across the dividing plane; the 45° mitered
abutments on both ends seat tightly under compression and avoid the
fracture-prone square shoulder with its short end grain at the lap tip.
Weak in tension unless pegged or bolted through the lap.

## Fabrication – sequence and tools
Tools: square, marking gauge, marking knife/pencil; Japanese pull saw
(ryoba: rip for the cheek, fine crosscuts for the bevels) or fine western
backsaw; chisel for cleanup; clamp/vise.

1. **Layout** – scribe lap length, dividing plane and both 45° bevels all
   the way around on all four faces (square + marking gauge). Clean layout
   decides everything here: both parts must be congruent.
2. **Cheek cut** – clamp the piece vertical, rip along the dividing plane
   down to the shoulder line.
3. **Tip miter** – piece horizontal; saw off the small 45° bevel at the
   lap tip (the small red wedge falls away).
4. **Shoulder miter** – crosscut the long 45° bevel at the lap root; the
   large wedge frees the waste and the lap is exposed.
5. **Cleanup** – pare cheek and both bevels flat and square with the
   chisel; work to the scribed lines, not past them.
6. **Assembly** – slide the two identical parts together lengthwise
   (A+A), check the seat; the miters pull the splice tight as it settles.
   Against tension, optionally drive wooden pegs or bolts through the lap.

## Effort
A few saw cuts per part, no mortising – one of the simplest splices,
well suited to cutting in situ.

## Provenance
Japanese ai-sogi-tsugi (相殺継ぎ), "Timber Joints" catalogue, chapter
Splicing Joints, pp. 158–159; European counterpart: gerades Blatt mit
schrägem Stoß (straight lap with beveled abutments).
