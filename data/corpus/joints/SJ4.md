# SJ4 – Undersquinted Spear Point Half Lap Joint

DE: Gerades Blatt mit schrägem Gratschnitt · JP: naga-koshi-kake-yahazu-tsugi (sogi-tsuki) · 長腰掛矢筈継ぎ（殺付）

SJ3's chevron and SJ2's undercut on the same joint. The lap is flat and at
mid-height; in plan each abutment is a chevron, giving one half a spear point
and the other a matching nock; in elevation both abutments lean out of square
by the undersquint. Both parts identical ("2×").

There is no gable, ridge or roof anywhere in this joint. *Gratschnitt* here
names the raking abutment cut, not a hipped surface.

## Cut structure

Six half-space cuts in three intersect groups:

| group | cuts | reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | lap_up, sh_a, sh_b | above the lap, beyond both shoulder flanks |
| 1 | lap_dn, tip_a | below the lap, beyond the +x tip flank |
| 2 | lap_dn, tip_b | below the lap, beyond the −x tip flank |

The shoulder is a **nock** — kept material forms a valley, so its two flanks
must be intersected, and the lap plane has to be intersected with them
because the undercut tilts the valley edge out of the vertical. That is why
group 0 has three members. The tongue tip is a **spear point** — kept
material comes to a ridge, so its flanks union, giving two separate groups
that share the lap slab.

Without the undercut this joint is SJ3 and needs no groups at all: the valley
edge would run vertically and a single prism profile could hold the corner.
The undersquint is what forces the intersection.

## One deliberate departure from the drawing

As in SJ2, the scan shows **square shoulders and undercut tips only** — the
shoulder transitions are one to two pixels wide, the tips rake over 50 px.
Assembled, that is a designed relief: bearing at the lap plane, clearance
toward the outer face.

`kept` and `prosthesis` are exact complements of one stock, so the model
cannot hold a void. It carries the undercut on **both** abutments: tips
exactly as measured, shoulders as their exact complements. Cut the tips to
the model and relieve the shoulders by a millimetre or two when fitting.

## Structural behaviour

The chevron resists racking sideways; the undercut resists lifting; the lap
carries shear. It is the most constrained joint of the four half laps, and
the only one where both plan and section are working.

Verified against the model: the spear point reaches y = 2.558 on the centre
line and 2.232 at the arris, a 0.33 overhang; the nock opens from y = 0.440
at the centre to 0.767 at the arris. Model chevron slope 0.666 against 0.667
measured, undercut 0.700 against 0.698.

Tension along the beam is still carried by the peg, not the joinery.

## Fabrication – sequence and tools

The book gives seven steps (A1–A7), the longest sequence among the half laps.
Tools: try square, sliding bevel, marking gauge, marking knife; ryoba;
paring chisel; mallet.

1. **Lay out** (A1) – lap plane right around; chevron centre line on both
   wide faces; abutment stations; bevel lines on both narrow faces.
2. **Shoulder crosscut** (A2) – down to the lap line, following the chevron
   in plan, so the saw tracks a V rather than a straight line.
3. **Cheek rip** (A3) – along the lap plane to free the bulk.
4. **Chevron flanks** (A4, A5) – saw each flank of the point and of the nock.
5. **Undercut** (A6) – rake both abutments to the bevel lines.
6. **Pare** (A7) – the nock apex is the fragile spot; work to the line from
   both sides and never lever the chisel against the point.
7. **Assemble** (A+A → AA) – identical parts, end for end, closing along the
   beam only.

## Effort

The most work of the four half laps: the chevron doubles the number of saw
cuts at each abutment and the undercut adds a raking cut to each. Still hand
tool work, but the layout has to be right in two views at once.
